Audio levels: the system volume and the maximums

How loud the unit plays, and how loud it can never play. Two layers:

  • Maximums, per output: the levels the hardware gain stages are put at while that output plays. The loudest the output is meant to go, set by a person (SYSTEM LEVELS, on the AUDIO page) and applied by the platform.
  • The system volume, per output: a software volume in dB that every program shares. 0 dB is the maximum, so the volume only ever turns down.

Protecting the speaker is the maximum: nothing in the SDK can make an output louder than the levels kept for it. There is no filter and no limiter.

Where they are kept and who decides which output plays is audio-page.md; the rest of the audio path is audio-device.md.

The layers

Layer Set by Kept in the unit's audio.json Moves while playing?
Maximum, per output: each playback level's value, with its range a person, in SYSTEM LEVELS and nowhere else endpoints.<output>.maximum no: applied when the output is chosen
System volume, per output, in dB Vol± anywhere, the AUDIO page, an app's own control endpoints.<output>.volume_db yes, ramped
The app's own mix the app yes

At the maximum, the samples reach the hardware unchanged and the hardware is at the loudest level that output is meant to play. So 0 dBFS out of AudioOut at 0 dB of volume is the loudest the output goes. An app's mix past full scale is hard-clipped at the conversion: the maximum is what full scale means.

Outputs (endpoints)

Each endpoint keeps its own volume and maximum: speaker, headphones, speaker+headphones, out (a unit that cannot tell which plays), builtin (a unit not set up), usb:<vid:pid>, card:<id>, default. Which one plays is decided by audiounit::endpointOf from the card playing, the unit's set-up, its Plays on and the socket (audio-page.md). All of them belong to the unit: a USB interface used on two units keeps a volume on each.

The value

  • dB, −60 to 0, then mute one press below −60. Up from mute is −60.
  • 1.5 dB a press. Steps in dB are the same size to the ear anywhere.
  • First time: 0 dB, except an output with headphones on it (headphones, or speaker + headphones), which starts at −18 dB. A first plug never delivers the speaker's level to someone's ears.
  • Kept to 0.1 dB, "muted" for mute.

Who applies it

The SDK (default) The app (Settings::appVolume)
The app's mix nominal, at 0 dB already has volumeGain() in it
Where AudioOut, after the render function and before the conversion, so the meter and the over count see what the device gets wherever the app puts it
A change of volume or endpoint ramped across one block the app's to smooth
The exact path (setExact, MDFourier) not applied: a measurement signal is not turned down not applied

Exactly one of the two applies it. The log says which, once, when the endpoint is found:

audio: volume of Speaker (RK3326-…/speaker): -6 dB, applied here (opened)

The API

include/sndstation/volume.h: Endpoint (the unit's key, the endpoint's name, a label), of(), current() (the endpoint a program would play on now, with no stream open), load(), save(), step(), gain(), position(), text(), and the constants.

On AudioOut, for the endpoint its stream plays on:

const volume::Endpoint& endpoint() const;  // .name: "speaker", "usb:16c0:048a", …
float volumeDb() const;                    // volume::MUTED when muted
float volumeGain() const;                  // linear, 0 when muted; RT-safe (an atomic)
float stepVolume(int dir);                 // Vol+ / Vol-: one step, saved at once
void  setVolumeDb(float db);               // clamped, saved
bool  refreshEndpoint();                   // decide again and apply (a plug, a change)

open() finds the endpoint and reads its volume before the first callback, and on its card applies what playing it writes: the built-in card's route and speaker switch, then the endpoint's maximum. Only what differs is written. service() (or followJack() alone), every frame, follows a card coming and going, and a plug: that swaps the route and the volume only.

Maximums

Set in SYSTEM LEVELS (AUDIO, under LEVELS), by ear: with the test sound on, pings at full scale, 0 dBFS, play past the volume there; the card's playback levels are listed first, each with a bar and its dB. Raise them until it distorts, rattles or is too loud, and back off. A playback level moved is kept at once, all of them together, as the playing output's maximum. On headphones the pings stay at the volume. Nothing else sets a maximum: not an app, not the SDK, not the volume. A board's preset carries the maximum the team set for its speaker this same way, and the unit's own, once set, replaces it (audio-page.md).

Why full scale: the maximum is what a full-scale sample produces, the loudest any app can make the output. Judged at −12 dBFS, as the first version did, a full-scale app plays 12 dB louder than anything heard while setting it.

Kept per level as { "control", "value", "range": [lo, hi] }, the value in the card's own units. A maximum level is refused, and said, where the card no longer has the control, or reports another range than the one it was kept in: set it again.

Applied by AudioOut when its stream opens, when the card changes and on refreshEndpoint, and at startup by applyStartupMixer(), after the levels kept by hand, so the maximum wins. Not on a headphone plug: that swaps the route and the volume, nothing else.

An output with no maximum, its own or its board's preset's, has none, and nothing is written on its behalf: its levels stay where the card has them.

Who uses which
Launcher Vol± on every page but CONTROLS step the volume of the output a program would play on (volume::current()), with an overlay saying which and how loud. With the launcher's stream playing (every page, launcher/audio.h), they step that stream's volume, heard at once.
AUDIO page the playing output's volume (User level) on the main list, and every output's on its own page; its maximum in SYSTEM LEVELS. Its test sound plays at the volume (as loud as an app at this volume); SYSTEM LEVELS (but not on headphones) and the set-up by ear play past it.
An app with a volume of its own applies volumeGain() itself (appVolume) where its own gain was, if it must come before something of its own, else leaves it to AudioOut; steps it with stepVolume().

Calibration signals: what is safe where

Signal Speaker Headphones, line out
Pings at full scale, with gaps (the test sound, at the volume; past it in SYSTEM LEVELS) raise the levels with care at the volume only; not on anyone's ears until it is down
Sparse bursts at full scale a quarter of the heat as above
Beeps at −18 dBFS, 20% of the time (the set-up by ear) safe to leave running at a working gain safe
Steady sine at −12 dBFS (the steady tone) warms the voice coil; not to be left on safe at modest gain
MDFourier (AUDIO's TOOLS) refused the measurement: one recording, analyzed on a desk (audio-device.md §2.4)

Power

The split of gain between hardware and software does not change the battery: the same loudness is the same power, whichever stage made it. What does: closing the stream when nothing sounds, larger buffers when not performing, and powering the unused output down (the route and the speaker switch). Expectations, not measurements.

Open questions

  1. Hiss at the lowest volumes on sensitive headphones, with the hardware at the maximum: measure before putting hardware steps under the software volume while silent.
  2. A calibration mode that steps a level between MDFourier runs and logs which step was where, to find a maximum by measurement.
  3. USB interfaces: their own playback level kept as a maximum, in place of the raise from 0% (raiseUsbVolumeOnce)?
  4. A quiet timeout, closing the stream after a while of silence.
  5. The overlay: drawn by the SDK, so every app looks the same?
  6. Programs outside the SDK get the maximum (it is in the mixer) but not the software volume. Deferred: they follow the SDK for now.
