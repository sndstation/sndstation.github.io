Gamepad names, prints and layout

Three things about every control on a pad: the one name the project uses for it, what is printed on it, and where it is. Built in the SDK, apart from choosing among several bodies that share one ID (below, "Which body this is").

In the SDK (pad.h, buttons.h):
pad::standardLabel(b) the standard name: "A" for East, always
pad::label(b) what the pad in use prints there; the standard name when it prints nothing known
pad::layout() the pad in use's layout: its name, frontW/frontH, and controls by key
pad::control(b), pad::control("LSTICK") one control's print, printAt, panel, and, when placed, x, y, w, h, rotation, shape

tests/test_layout.cpp checks all of it on virtual pads.

Names: one standard, by position

Docs, code and config call the face buttons by the Nintendo/handheld letters, and each letter means a position, whatever is printed there:

name position pad:: config key old bit name
A East East EAST JOY_BUTTON1
B South South SOUTH JOY_BUTTON2
X North North NORTH JOY_BUTTON3
Y West West WEST JOY_BUTTON4

The rest are named as they are: Up, Down, Left, Right (the d-pad), L1, L2, R1, R2, L3, R3 (the stick clicks), Start, Select, Menu, Vol+, Vol−, and the sticks LX, LY, RX, RY.

So "A" in a doc is the East button on every pad, including an Xbox pad that prints A on the South one and a unit that prints something else entirely. A doc that tells a person what to press names the position the first time: "A (East)".

This is a name, not a print. What the screen shows is the print.

Prints: what is on the button

Each control in a layout (below) can say what is printed on or next to it, exactly as written, in UTF-8: "A", "✕", "△", "SELECT", "−", "☰", "TL". It cannot give the print's color: Control has no field for one (see Open). A control with nothing printed has "".

pad::label(b) returns the print when the unit's layout is known, and the standard name otherwise. The launcher and every app that asks the SDK show the right print with no change of their own. An app that writes "A" itself has chosen the standard name; that is its call.

A pad with no layout in the database still gets its prints when SDL knows its family: an Xbox pad (360, One, Series) prints A at the bottom, B, X and Y round it, and a PlayStation pad ✕ ○ □ △. That is prints only, with no geometry, and a layout in the database always wins. A Nintendo pad prints the standard names already.

Layout: where things are

A new table, "layouts", beside "maps" in gamepads.json, named like the maps. A device entry points to one with "layout": "<name>". A map says which SDL button is which position; a layout says what that position looks like on this body. They are separate because one map serves several bodies.

The XU-10 (MagicX/COOPREME, RK3326) is the case that shows why. It is Xbox-lettered, so every face button prints something other than its standard name. Its Menu button is a "G" on the right-hand edge, under the headphone socket, and its shoulders are on the back, not the top. Measured from photos of each side, scaled to its 130 mm height:

What an app learns from it:

  • A doc's "A (East)" is the button printed B, and the screen says B.
  • Start's center is 14.5 mm from X's; Select's is 22 mm from X and 16 mm from the d-pad. Start is the one a thumb on the face buttons finds.
  • The shoulders are on the back, 45 mm down, under the fingers that hold it, not on the top edge.
  • G is on the right edge, 29 mm down: reached by moving the right hand, not by a thumb.

The rules:

  • Millimeters, on the front as the user holds it: origin at the top left, x to the right, y down. A control is placed by its center (at) and its bounding size, with shape one of round, rect or pill, and an optional clockwise rotation in degrees. Photos of known size are good enough; ±2 mm does not change an ergonomic answer.
  • front_mm is the size of the front.
  • The sticks are LSTICK and RSTICK; their clicks, L3 and R3, are where the stick is and need no place of their own.
  • Other panels: "panel" is back, top, bottom, left or right (the front is the default). Every panel uses the front's x and y as seen from the front, so L1 is on the left wherever it is: the back is seen through the unit, not from behind. An edge has no extent in one of them and uses the depth from the front face instead: the top and bottom are [x, depth], the sides [depth, y].
  • print_at says where a print is when it is beside the control rather than on it: above, below, left or right.
  • The headphone socket may be placed as well ("HEADPHONES"), with no print: a cable coming out of an edge is part of how the unit is held.
  • A control the layout leaves out has no position, and an app must not assume one.
  • A device with no layout has no geometry: its prints are its SDL family's, as above, or the standard names.
  • A user's own gamepads.json (the one the CONTROLS mapper writes) replaces the shipped file, and one saved before layouts existed has none. So the shipped file's layouts are always read too: a device whose entry has no "layout" takes the shipped entry's, and a layout name is looked up in the user's file first, then the shipped one.

An app can then ask what it needs. For example, a music app can ask whether Select is close enough to the face buttons that it gets pressed by accident, and leave it out of a modifier on such a body.

Which body this is

An external pad is its device entry: vid, pid and name.

The built-in controls belong to the unit, and one ID does not always mean one body: clones share USB IDs and a device tree, and differ in prints and placement. Where that is known, the device entry lists the candidates, "layout": ["r36s-a", "r36s-b"]. CONTROLS asks once, with a drawing of each, and keeps the choice for this unit (unit-identity.md), as the audio profile is kept.

Not built yet: the list is read, and its first layout is used (the log says so). The CONTROLS question is still to come.

CLAP

The fields are those of an element in the embedded-hardware draft (clap_extensions/embedded-hardware.h, clap.embedded-hardware/1), so a host fills the draft straight from the layout (not done yet: the host still reports one panel with no geometry):

layout draft element / panel
print label
nothing: a layout has no print color label_color, left unset
at x_mm, y_mm
size width_mm, height_mm
rotation rotation_deg
shape shape
print_at label_position
panel panel_id (front, back, an edge)
front_mm the front panel's width_mm, height_mm
no layout NO_GEOMETRY

The draft places panels on "the device's front" only. It has no word for the back or an edge, and the XU-10 has controls on both: a point to raise with the draft. The headphone socket is the draft's two-position SWITCH.
