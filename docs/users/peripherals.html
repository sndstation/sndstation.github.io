Peripherals: what works on your unit

For: someone about to plug something into a handheld and wanting to know whether it will work before buying it.

What a unit supports depends on its chip, not on its brand name. Find your family in the table: the device guide and Identifying your unit say which one you have.

RK3326 RK3566 H700 TrimUI (A133) PlayStation Classic
Audio interface, MIDI keyboard yes yes yes yes yes
USB stick, card reader yes yes yes yes yes
exFAT and NTFS drives yes yes yes no no
USB-serial adapter yes yes yes yes yes
Game controller yes yes yes basic only basic only
Bluetooth yes yes built-in only yes no
Wi-Fi dongle yes yes yes, 0.15.1-h700 or later not needed required
Webcam no no add-on no no

Models in each family: RK3326 is the RG351 series, R33S/R36S and RGB10; RK3566 is the RG353 series, RGB30, RK2023 and X55; H700 is the RG35XX series, RG40XX, RG34XX, RG28XX and RGcubeXX; A133 is the TrimUI Smart Pro, Brick and Brick Pro.

Audio and MIDI

Every unit takes a class-compliant USB audio interface or MIDI controller, with MIDI 2.0. Class-compliant means the device needs no vendor driver, which is most interfaces made in the last decade and nearly every MIDI keyboard.

Interfaces that need a vendor driver do not work, and are not planned: Native Instruments, Line 6, and the Edirol and Roland units that use their own protocols. If a device's manual tells you to install a driver on a PC, it will not work here.

Storage

USB sticks, card readers and USB drives work on every unit. On the TrimUI units and the PlayStation Classic, format the drive FAT32 or ext4: those two kernels have no exFAT or NTFS support, so a drive formatted the way it came out of the shop may not mount. The other three families read exFAT and NTFS as they are.

Controllers

Every unit handles a generic USB or Bluetooth gamepad. Console pads that need their own driver are patchier: Xbox pads work on RK3326 and RK3566, PlayStation pads on RK3566 and the H700, and Nintendo and Steam pads only on RK3326. The TrimUI units and the PlayStation Classic have generic support only, so a console pad may show up with its buttons mapped oddly or not at all.

Bluetooth

RK3326 and RK3566 take a Bluetooth USB dongle as well as using whatever is built in. The H700 can use its own Bluetooth but not a dongle. The TrimUI units have Bluetooth on the same chip as their Wi-Fi. The PlayStation Classic has no Bluetooth at all.

Wi-Fi

Most units have Wi-Fi of their own and need nothing. Two cases do not:

  • The RG28XX has no Wi-Fi, so a USB dongle is its only way onto a network. Use a Realtek 8188EU, 8188CU/8192CU or 8192EU dongle; these are the cheap ones sold for the Raspberry Pi.
  • The PlayStation Classic likewise needs a dongle, and the supported one is a Realtek RTL8821CU.

Other chipsets are not supported, so check the chipset and not the brand before buying. Dongle Wi-Fi also needs the matching firmware, which the images carry.

On the H700, the image has to be recent enough. The driver arrived in the 0.15.1-h700 release; the H700 image in 0.15.1 came before it, and a dongle does nothing there. Take the H700 image from 0.15.1-h700 or later.

Watch the filename when you do. An image is named for the date it was built, not for the release it came from, so the H700 image in 0.15.1 and the one in 0.15.1-h700 are both called sndstation-H700-DDR4-20261001.img.xz and are not the same image. If you have downloaded both they will collide in one folder: keep them apart, or check each against the .sha256 beside it in the release.

Webcams

No unit supports a USB webcam out of the box. On the H700 the driver can be installed as an add-on package afterwards, because that kernel can load modules and already has the video framework built in. On the other families it cannot be added after the fact: the TrimUI and PlayStation Classic kernels load no modules at all, and the RK3326 and RK3566 would need a kernel rebuild.

Webcams are not in the kernels because no supported unit needs one to do anything it is meant to do. A driver goes in when a unit would otherwise be unable to do something basic, which is why the Wi-Fi dongle drivers above are there.

If something does not work

Peripheral support in the table is what the kernel offers, not a promise that every device in a class has been tried. If a device in a supported class does not work, say what the unit is and what lsusb reports for the device.
